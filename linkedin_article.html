Recently, Kai published a fantastic video titled *"llama.cpp Just Made 125B MoE Models 4× Faster!"* analyzing the newly introduced MoE expert cache (--moe-cache-mib from PR #29887 and the recent Oct 19 SLRU cache PR #30637). The premise sounded incredible: keep non-fitting MoE experts in system RAM and dynamically cache hot experts in GPU VRAM to unlock 4× speedups on consumer hardware. Naturally, we put this to the ultimate test. We compiled the absolute latest llama.cpp master branch with CUDA 13.1 on an RTX 5060 Ti 16GB (32GB DDR5 RAM) and benchmarked the official 24GB NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Bolt (74B total / 9B active parameters). Here is what happened—and why Kai’s warning around the 23:17 mark (“Prompts, drafts and loops”) proved to be the exact Achilles’ heel of reactive caches. ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 💥 The Benchmark: RTX 5060 Ti 16GB Model: NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-74B-A9B (20.5 GB GGUF / 25 GB Raw) Prompt: "Explain the difference between SSD and RAM in simple terms." ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 1️⃣ llama.cpp Full Offload (-ngl 88): Result: < 1.0 tok/s. Total GPU starvation. WDDM 3.x shared memory paging causes constant PCIe thrashing. GPU utilization dropped to 9% at 20 Watts. 2️⃣ llama.cpp New MoE Cache (--moe-cache-mib 6000): Result: 1.7 tok/s prefill | 3.5 tok/s decode. Wait—why did the 4× speedup collapse? Nemotron 3.5 Bolt features 496 routed experts per layer across 40 layers = 19,840 experts! A 6GB VRAM cache holds only ~22% of them. On every single cache miss, llama.cpp halts execution (cudaStreamWaitEvent) and stalls the Tensor Cores waiting for PCIe transfers. 3️⃣ llama.cpp Partial CPU Offload (-ngl 48): Result: 5.6 tok/s prefill | 6.9 tok/s decode. Offloading 40 layers to CPU beat the MoE cache, but burned 100% of all CPU cores via AVX. 4️⃣ MinnieTheMoEcher (Our Bare-Metal CUDA Engine): Result: 21.54 tok/s prefill | 13.51 tok/s decode | 0% CPU Load! 📊 The Scorecard: • Decode Speed: 13.51 tok/s vs 3.5 tok/s (3.86× faster than llama.cpp’s MoE cache) • Prefill Speed: 21.54 tok/s vs 1.7 tok/s (12.6× faster) • Hardware Load: 100% GPU Tensor Cores, 0% CPU core burn (vs 100% CPU lockup on llama.cpp) ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ 🔍 Why Minnie Crushes Reactive Caches ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ As Kai pinpointed at 23:17, reactive LRU/SLRU caches suffer from the "synchronous wait-on-miss" flaw. When your expert cardinality is high (like 19,840 experts), miss penalties obliterate throughput. Minnie solves this through a completely different paradigm: ✅ Asynchronous Double-Buffered DMA: While Layer N is executing on GPU Tensor Cores (computing Mamba-2 SSM and dense Attention), Layer N+1’s required experts are pre-streamed in parallel over dedicated copy streams. Transfer latency is completely hidden behind computation. ✅ Zero Driver Thrashing: Host weights reside in pinned, non-pageable DRAM (L2 tier), bypassing OS page faults. ✅ Pure GPU Compute: Every single multiply-accumulate runs on Tensor Cores. Your CPU stays 100% free for your OS and application stack. Massive 70B+ MoE inference on a single sub-$500 consumer GPU isn't a pipe dream—it just requires eliminating synchronous PCIe stalls.
